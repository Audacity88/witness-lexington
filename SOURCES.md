Public sources below were read on October 9, 2026, after read-only network access was enabled. Each entry gives the passage location, what it supports, and its limits. The product spec and private BrainLift are not historical evidence. Ellipses mark trims in primary-source quotations. A passage summary is a paraphrase, not historical dialogue.

## Night alarm and escape

`[NIGHT]`: Lexington History Museums, [*Paul Revere's Ride*, April 18th, 1775 section](https://www.lexingtonhistory.org/paulrevere). The paragraphs beginning "British soldiers in Boston", "Revere arrived in Lexington", and "Revere made his way back" describe the warning, Adams and Hancock lodging at Clarke's parsonage, and their departure to safety. The last passage includes:

> …Revere personally escorted the men to a nearby safe house shortly before dawn…

The museum identifies later recollections among its sources. Its arrival time differs from the NPS timeline's 12:30 a.m. entry, so the game uses the night of April 18 to 19 without an exact arrival time. Eli's route, horse, errands, encounters, and departure staging remain visibly dramatized. The game does not quote a reconstructed parsonage conversation as primary testimony.

Barker's diary excerpt in NPS Reading 2, cited under `[BARKER]` below, puts the people formed on the Common at about 200 to 300 (printed as "2 and 300"). That is substantially higher than Gould's, Revere's, and Willard's militia estimates. Attribute it to Barker rather than silently replace it with 80.

Willard's chosen transcription reads:

> …saw (as I suppose) about four hundred regulars in one body…

The cited eyewitness estimates of approaching regulars span about 400 to 1,000 (Willard and Robins); NPS describes about 200 in the detached advance force and 700 in the whole expedition. The cited militia estimates span about 50 to 300, with Barker at the high end; NPS gives 77. Force scopes and observations differ. ## Formation

`[FORMATION]`: [*Battles of Lexington and Concord*, Lexington section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord#Lexington), paragraph beginning "Parker positioned his company". Its passage describes parade-ground formation in plain sight, rather than concealment behind walls, and cites John R. Galvin, *The Minute Men*, pages 120 to 124. This is a checked public secondary passage; Galvin's book was not independently consulted.

A supporting primary account is the April 25, 1775 joint deposition of Nathaniel Parkhurst, Jonas Parker, John Munroe Jr., and eleven other named deponents, in the [Tomlinson supplement, printed pages 103 to 104](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20636/20636-h/20636-h.htm#page103). It describes the company forming on the parade facing the approaching regulars, then dispersing. It is a different document from John Parker's deposition below. Exact model spacing remains a reconstruction.

## Commands and named-person movements

`[COMMAND]`: [the public Lexington section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord#Lexington), paragraph beginning "British Marine Lieutenant Jesse Adair". It describes Adair leading the advance companies onto the Common and states:

> Major Pitcairn then arrived from the rear of the advance force.

That paragraph cites David Hackett Fischer, *Paul Revere's Ride*, 1994, pages 189 to 190. The next paragraph describes shouting, Parker's raspy command, and some men not hearing it. It cites Sanderson and Willard depositions and the *Journals of the Continental Congress*, May 11, 1775. This packet cites the accessible secondary passage for those details, not an independently checked Fischer page or a primary medical diagnosis. Dialogue and performed voice volume remain dramatized. Willard's primary account above also says he did not understand an officer's words; this supports the hearing task without identifying that officer as Parker.

## Outcome

`[OUTCOME]`: NPS, [*April 19, 1775*, 5:00 a.m. entry](https://www.nps.gov/mima/learn/historyculture/april-19-1775.htm), describes eight militia killed and ten wounded after volleys and a bayonet rush. [*Battles of Lexington and Concord*, British advance to Concord section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord#British_advance_to_Concord), first paragraph, supplies the single British soldier wounded and cites Benjamin Quarles, *The Negro in the American Revolution*, page 10. This is a checked public secondary passage; Quarles's book was not independently consulted. These figures concern Lexington, not all fighting on April 19.

## Officers' control and Barker's account

`[CONTROL]` and `[BARKER]`: NPS, [*Embattled Farmers*, Reading 2: More Than One Side to Every Story](https://www.nps.gov/articles/embattled-farmers-and-the-shot-heard-round-the-world-the-battles-of-lexington-and-concord-teaching-with-historic-places.htm), Lt. John Barker's diary excerpt, April 19 entry. NPS cites "Diary of a British Soldier", *Atlantic Monthly*, April 1877, volume 39. The relevant trimmed passage is:

> …they fired one or two shots, upon which our Men without any orders rushed in upon them…

Barker attributes the first shots to the militia and describes the subsequent rush as occurring without orders. This is his testimony, not a settled first-shot finding. Use this NPS excerpt for Barker rather than the Wikipedia summary's narrower shot count. The [public British advance to Concord section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord#British_advance_to_Concord), paragraph beginning "Pitcairn's companies", describes officers losing control and later restoring order, citing Fischer, pages 198 to 200. That checked secondary passage supports the control fact card. No private motive or officer's first-shot order is asserted.

## The specific militia deposition

`[MILITIA]`: John Parker, *Deposition of Captain John Parker Concerning the Battle at Lexington*, April 25, 1775, document No. 4. [DocsTeach transcript and image](https://docsteach.org/document/john-parker-lexington/); [National Archives identifier 595246](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/595246). Collection: Massachusetts State Papers, Papers of the Continental Congress, 1774 to 1789, Record Group 360, National Archives, Washington, DC. The dated transcript, document number, and full citation were checked on DocsTeach. The National Archives catalog link is its supplied record locator.

Parker says he ordered dispersal and no firing, then attributes an unprovoked attack to the regulars. The chosen transcript includes:

> …I immediately ordered our Militia to disperse and not to fire…

> …without receiving any Provocation therefor from us…

Label the inherited `depositions` card "John Parker's sworn deposition, April 25, 1775; summary". Preserve its scoring ID. Its summary supports the militia account while the opposing Barker account remains available. This is Parker's claim, not a settled verdict. Do not substitute the separate joint deposition or imply Parker witnessed every possible shooter.

## Revere transcription choices

`[R75]`: [MHS item 98, transcript](https://www.masshist.org/database/viewer.php?item_id=98&mode=transcript#page3), manuscript page 3. The displayed transcription was retrieved directly from MHS. It describes hearing a report, turning, seeing smoke in front of the troops, and being unable to see the militia because of a house. A trimmed example is:

> …one gun was fired, I heard the report, turned my head…

The house statement is part of a longer sentence, so do not present the seed's house fragment as a complete sentence without ellipses.

`[R98]`: Paul Revere's letter to Jeremy Belknap, 1798, manuscript held by MHS as item 99. Choose the [MASSAR transcription](https://massar.org/a-letter-from-col-paul-revere-to-the-corresponding-secretary-jeremy-belknap-on-the-battle-of-lexington/), paragraph beginning "In their Front", consistently retaining `&`. Its text includes:

> …I saw, & heard, a Gun fired, which appeared to be a Pistol.

The MASSAR page visually marks the whole paragraph as struck through. Retain that editorial flag when displaying its excerpt. The [MHS manuscript transcription](https://www.masshist.org/database/viewer.php?item_id=99&mode=transcript#page6) was also retrieved and includes the passage, but is not the chosen quotation transcription for the 1798 card. Both accounts come from Revere; the later letter is not independent confirmation. Neither establishes who pulled a trigger.
